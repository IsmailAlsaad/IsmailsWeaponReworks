# Ismail's Weapon Reworks: Roadmap

Standalone tModLoader mod (internal name `IsmailsWeaponReworks`) with reworks of vanilla Terraria weapons. It depends on no other mod. Factorraria lists it in `modReferences`. Each weapon gets its own section; one chat = one patch (or a few small patches of the same weapon). Vanilla items are reworked through a `GlobalItem` (recipe, drops and base stats stay vanilla). Vanilla dust and sounds only; Ismail provides all art (every sprite has a vanilla fallback until his file exists). Multiplayer first: the owner's client decides, everyone else mirrors.

Design preferences: lots of particles and visuals with trails and sound effects and animations and all the satisfying game juice.

Layout: one folder per weapon, `Content/Weapons/<Weapon>/` (GlobalItem, projectiles, Tuning, Fx, art). Namespace always equals the folder path. Shared code goes into `Common/` only when a second weapon needs it.

## 1. Ice Boomerang (vanilla `ItemID.IceBoomerang`) [DONE]

Throw it and it becomes a crystal; hit a crystal and it chains through all of them, exploding each. Only the one-line stub of the original design text survived in Factorraria's repo; restore the design details here if you want them kept.

Multiplayer review (2026-11-01, code review only, not yet tested with two players): the owner's client decides everything (throw, stop point, crystal spawn and eviction, chain targets, blast, chill); other machines run the same motion and the visuals from synced ai values and netUpdate. NPC chill goes through vanilla `NPC.AddBuff`, which syncs unless `quiet` is set. No functional desync found. R2: hit sparks (`OnHitNPC` only runs on the owner's client) are now also shown to the other players: every other machine runs the same swept hit test on its own copy of the enemies and spawns the sparks itself (visual only, no packets, once per enemy per segment). Known cosmetic gap, left as it is: a player who joins late sees each existing crystal with a fresh 30 s timer.

## 2. Ice Blade (vanilla `ItemID.IceBlade`) [patches 2-4 applied, untested]

Design: tap the attack button = plain custom melee chop (no wave, no cooldown). Hold = charge: ice-crack clicks that rise in pitch, one per tier (ticks 18 / 36 / 54), then a 4th low click at tick 68. Releasing after click 1, 2 or 3 = slash wave of that tier; releasing between click 3 and click 4 is the sweet spot. Still holding at click 4 = vanilla `BuffID.Frozen` for 3 s (roots the player and blocks every item); no wave fires. Click timing is fixed ticks, not scaled by melee speed.

Look: `IceBladeSwingProjectile` is a held projectile that draws the vanilla blade sprite. The arm lifts with a cubic ease-out (blade tilt in `IceBladeTuning`) and reaches full height on click 3; the blade trembles before click 4. Every click flashes and glows the blade and bursts dust. Release = the exact same chop as the tap swing (one shared timeline), with a freezing trail behind the blade (thinner and fainter for lower tiers); only the wave flies toward the cursor, starting in front of the player halfway out along the blade's reach. The trail is a pixel line along the blade's edge (`IceBladeTrailStyle`, a `PixelTrail` with one point on the tip, three per tick, drawn by `PixelLine`): a wide faint glow layer under a narrow bright core, tapering toward its tail, and it is handed to `DetachedTrails` when the swing projectile dies so it keeps fading on its own. `IceBladeWaveProjectile` is a crescent drawn from pixel squares (thick middle, tapering tips, afterimages, halo, blue Ice-dust fog). It expands, slows down and fades but its damage stays constant; it passes through blocks and hits each enemy once. Tier damage multipliers 0.55 / 1.0 / 1.8. All numbers are in `IceBladeTuning`, all dust and sound in `IceBladeFx`. Shared dust helpers were moved to `Common/IceFx.cs` (`IceBoomerangFx` forwards to them).

Patch 4 (applied, untested): the release swing's trail is no longer a line along the tip but an EXPANDING FAN. `IceBladeTrailStyle` is now a `SweepTrailStyle` and the swing projectile lays a `SweepTrail` (new `Common/SweepTrail.cs`, handed to `DetachedTrails` like the other trails): every sample is a pair of points, the tip and an inner point on the blade, three samples per tick. The inner point starts at the tip and slides down the blade toward the hand as the swing builds (smoothstep, full size when 85% of the swing is done), so the trail begins as a thin line at the top of the blade and widens to the bottom of it into a fan. The fan is drawn as 7 parallel pixel lines between the two edges (bright core on the tip edge, mid blue, halo blue toward the hand, fainter toward the hand) and fades from its tail. Tier 1 / 2 / 3 reach 45% / 65% / 88% of the way down the blade and keep their opacity; the tuning is `TrailInnerFraction`, `TrailExpandBy`, `TrailRails`, `TrailOpacity` (the old `TrailThickness` is gone). Test: a charged release leaves a fan that begins at the tip and widens toward the hand as the blade swings, bigger and brighter for higher tiers, with no gaps between its lines at full speed; it keeps fading after the swing ends; the tap swing still has no trail; facing left and with the cursor on both sides works; two players: the other player sees the same fan.

Multiplayer design: the owner's client decides release, tier, aim, freeze and the wave spawn, and syncs them through the held projectile's ai[0] phase, ai[1] tier, ai[2] aim angle (netUpdate). Every machine runs the same timers for the arm pose, click sounds, flash, dust and trail. The wave is a normal spawned projectile (synced by tModLoader, hit by the owner's client). The Frozen buff is added by the owner's client only. No custom packets.

Art: none needed (vanilla blade sprite and the 1x1 magic pixel).

Test checklist: tap swings and damages; holding plays 3 rising clicks then a low 4th; release before click 1 = chop only; release after click 1/2/3 = three clearly different wave sizes and damage; release between click 3 and 4 = biggest; holding past click 4 freezes the player about 3 s with no wave; swapping items mid-charge ends cleanly; waves stop at walls; the swing leaves one thin pixel-line trail along the blade tip (thicker and brighter for higher tiers) that fades smoothly from its tail, also after the swing is over; the wave's afterimages keep fading on their own when the wave ends; the arm pose is right facing left and with the cursor on both sides; two players: the other player sees the arm lift, clicks, flash, slash, trail and wave, and the wave's hits register for the owner.

## 3. Enchanted Boomerang (vanilla `ItemID.EnchantedBoomerang`) [DONE, patches 1-2 tested; afterimages patch 3 untested]

Design: left click throws the normal boomerang, right click (`AltFunctionUse`) throws an ethereal one drawn see-through with a ghostly trail. One of each kind can be out at the same time (`EnchantedBoomerangProjectile` and `EtherealBoomerangProjectile` are two types sharing `EnchantedBoomerangBase`, so `ownedProjectileCounts` limits each kind separately). Both fly a fixed range (18 tiles, tunable) and return to the player; a block ends the way out and turns them back (both kinds, the ethereal one does NOT pass blocks); they pierce infinitely (`penetrate = -1`, an enemy can be hit again after 12 ticks, and again on the way back). Same damage for both.

Collision: when a normal and an ethereal one touch (swept test on their relative motion, 20 px radius each, forgiving on purpose; once per throw), `StardustBurstProjectile` explodes at the contact point (damages every enemy in 72 px once, 1.5x boomerang damage, gold and blue sparkle dust plus two pixel rings), and each boomerang gets a sideways impulse (13 speed, 90 degrees to its own travel direction, on the side that leads away from the other one). There is no separate bounce phase: it goes straight into the return. The return is force-based (`BoomerangFlight.StepForceReturn`, tuning `ReturnAcceleration` 1, `ReturnDrag` 0.95, `ReturnMaxSpeed` 18): the pull toward the player is added to the velocity it already has, so a normal return after the range end or a block, and a return after a bounce, are both one smooth arc that bends toward wherever the player is now. Phase value 2 (`Bounced`) flies exactly like 1 (`Returning`); it only marks that the bounce sparks were played and that this one is done colliding. Head-on throws (right click along the line the left boomerang is coming back on) are the easy way to trigger it.

Multiplayer design: both boomerangs belong to the same player, so the owner's client sees both and decides everything (turn, wall hit, collision, bounce, burst spawn); it syncs through ai[0] phase, velocity and netUpdate. Other machines simulate the same motion and visuals, and the burst is a normal spawned projectile that every machine plays on its first tick. Other players' hit sparks come from `Common/RemoteHitTracker` (extracted from the Ice Boomerang's R2 code, which now uses it too). No custom packets.

Art: optional. Drop `EnchantedBoomerangProjectile.png` and `EtherealBoomerangProjectile.png` into `Content/Weapons/EnchantedBoomerang/` (same size as the vanilla boomerang sprite, single frame) and they replace the vanilla texture automatically.

Test checklist: left click throws the normal one and right click the ethereal one, one of each at a time; thrown into open space it brakes at 18 tiles and comes back in a smooth curve (no straight-line homing, no circling the player) and is caught; thrown at a wall it turns back and does not slide through the wall; running away from a returning boomerang it still catches up; when the two meet there is the stardust burst and the sparks, both curve away from each other sideways and then swing back to the player in one smooth arc, with no stop-and-go; each bounces only once per throw; both are still caught; enemies can be hit again on the way back; two players: the other player sees the same arcs, the burst and the hit sparks.

Test checklist: left click throws and returns, right click throws the ghost version, both can be out together; each is limited to one in flight; both turn back at walls; piercing hits an enemy going out and coming back; head-on collision bursts in stardust at the meeting point and sends the boomerangs off sideways (apart from each other), then both return and are caught; a pair collides only once per throw; the burst damages nearby enemies once; Ice Boomerang still behaves the same (chain, crystals, chill) and still shows hit sparks to the other player; two players: the other player sees both boomerangs, the bounce, the burst and the hit sparks.

## 4. Trimarang (vanilla `ItemID.Trimarang`) [patches 1-2 tested; patch 3 applied, untested]

Design: hold left click. The boomerang flies toward the cursor, weakly steering toward it as it goes (at most 0.02 rad per tick), and glides to a stop after exactly 20 tiles of flight path (the speed is proportional to the distance left; steering only bends the direction), passing through blocks, and then spins there until the button is released (it also comes back if the item is swapped, or the player gets 45 tiles away). It spins slowly at first and speeds up (ease-in) to a cap over 3 s, and the faster it spins the shorter its per-enemy hit cooldown (14 to 5 ticks), so it shreds. Knockback is cut to 30% while holding so enemies stay in the blades. Release at any time (even before it reaches 20 tiles) and it accelerates back and is caught. While holding it also drifts slowly toward the cursor (3 px per tick at most), never farther than 20 tiles from the player (at the range it slides along the circle). The spin sheds stardust (the gold, white, blue and pink dusts) that flies outward while spiraling in the spin direction.

Ethereal forms: every 10th hit of the main boomerang (ethereal hits do not count) it sheds a `TrimarangEtherealProjectile`: a see-through copy that spins at the same speed, is shot outward (consecutive ones fan out by the golden angle), and homes on the nearest enemy within 30 tiles that is NOT the one that triggered it: the owner picks the first target when the ghost is spawned (so every machine has it from the spawn packet) and re-picks every 10 ticks when it is lost; with a target it flies straight out for 10 ticks, then steers at it (speed 15). It never targets and never damages the enemy that triggered it. When no other enemy is near it falls back to the arc: the shot is an impulse (speed 11) and a pull toward the player (`BoomerangFlight.StepForceReturn`, tuning `EtherealReturnAcceleration` 1, `EtherealReturnDrag` 0.95, `EtherealReturnMaxSpeed` 18) bends it into one smooth arc back, and reaching the player ends it with the same harmless explosion as the last hit; a 6 s safety limit remains. It passes through tiles, pierces, deals 0.7x damage, and after 3 hits it explodes as a visual only (no damage). At most 8 are alive at once (the oldest fizzles).

Multiplayer design: the owner's client decides everything (stop, wall, release, hit counting, ethereal spawns, target choice, the 3-hit explosion). The main boomerang syncs its phase with ai[0] and netUpdate; its spin is a pure function of its age, so every machine spins it the same. Steering: `Main.MouseWorld` exists only on the owner's client, so the owner bends the direction (Outbound) or drifts (Hold) and sends position and velocity through a throttled `netUpdate` (every `SteerSyncTicks` = 5 ticks, and only when the velocity changed); the other machines follow the synced velocity in between and are corrected at each sync. An ethereal form syncs its target and its "dying" state through ai[1] (>= 0 target NPC, -1 none, -2 dying): the owner picks the first target in the spawn and later ones by netUpdate, every machine steers toward the synced target or, with none, flies the same force-based arc from the synced launch velocity, and the owner decides the end (3 hits, reaching the player in the arc case, the safety limit), so every machine plays the explosion without a kill packet. Other players' hit sparks come from `Common/RemoteHitTracker`. No custom packets. The return and catch step moved to `Common/BoomerangFlight.cs` (the Enchanted Boomerang uses it too).

Needs the Enchanted Boomerang patch applied first.

Art: optional. Drop `TrimarangProjectile.png` and `TrimarangEtherealProjectile.png` into `Content/Weapons/Trimarang/` (same size as the vanilla Trimarang sprite, single frame).

Test checklist: hold left click: it flies out, bends gently toward the cursor if you move it, and glides to a stop about 20 tiles of path out; it passes through walls instead of stopping at them; while holding it spins, getting faster, with a rising whoosh, and drifts slowly toward the cursor but never farther than 20 tiles from you; the spin sheds gold, white, blue and pink stardust that flies outward while curving in the spin direction; releasing at any time (also before it arrives) sends it back and it is caught; it shreds enemies without knocking them away; every 10th hit sheds a ghost copy that is shot out and then homes on the nearest OTHER enemy within 30 tiles, never targeting or hurting the enemy that triggered it; a ghost explodes harmlessly after 3 hits; when no other enemy is near, the ghost arcs back to you instead and explodes harmlessly when it reaches you; ghosts pass through walls; ghosts that lose their target (it died) look for another one or arc back; no more than 8 ghosts at once; the Enchanted Boomerang still works; two players: the other player sees the main boomerang follow the steering without big jumps, the spin dust, the ghost arcs, their explosions and the hit sparks.

## 5. Flamerang (vanilla `ItemID.Flamarang`) [patch 2 applied, untested]

Design: it behaves like the Ice Boomerang. Left click throws it at the cursor (fixed 20-tile range, brakes at the end, only one thrown at a time) and it pierces everything, but it never returns as itself.
- Nothing hit: at the end of its range it spins down, then explodes in fire (`FlamerangBlastProjectile`: 96 px circle, boomerang damage, sets enemies on fire) and two `MoltenBoomerangProjectile`s fly off sideways from the explosion (an impulse) and are pulled back toward the player's current position by a force every tick, a smooth arc that follows the player if they moved (`BoomerangFlight.StepForceReturn`: acceleration, drag, max speed).
- Touching an enemy or a tile first: the same fire explosion happens at the contact point and also throws 6 `ObsidianShardProjectile`s (fan out upward, fall, land on the first tile and linger up to 8 s, cutting enemies that touch them; they glow like lava and cool to dark obsidian). A shard breaks in a shatter effect after 4 hits, when its time runs out, or when newer shards push it out: at most 15 per player, the oldest break first. The boomerang is replaced by one molten boomerang that returns straight to the player.
- Molten boomerangs pierce infinitely (an enemy can be hit again after 12 ticks and on the way back) and leave a trail of fire for 40 ticks; any enemy touching the trail is set on fire (vanilla On Fire, 4 s, refreshed below 1.5 s left).

Multiplayer design: the owner's client decides everything (stop, contact, explosion and every spawn) and flips the main boomerang's phase with ai[0] and netUpdate. The blast, the shards and the molten boomerangs are normal spawned projectiles, so tModLoader syncs them; every machine plays the blast visuals on its first tick. The molten flight is deterministic from its spawn velocity and the player's position (every machine runs the same force step); the owner applies the burning from the trail (`NPC.AddBuff` syncs itself). Every machine draws its own copy of the trail from where its copy has been. A shard's landing is decided by the owner (ai[0] = 1, netUpdate); its lifetime is a plain countdown each machine runs. Shard hits are counted by the owner (`OnHitNPC` only runs there); breaking (4th hit or eviction when more than 15 exist) is decided by the owner and synced with ai[1] = -2 and netUpdate, and every machine plays the shatter in `OnKill`, which also covers the lifetime ending. Other players' hit sparks come from `Common/RemoteHitTracker`. No custom packets.

Needs the Enchanted Boomerang and Trimarang patches applied first (it uses `Common/RemoteHitTracker` and `Common/BoomerangFlight`).

Art: optional. Drop `FlamerangProjectile.png`, `MoltenBoomerangProjectile.png` and `ObsidianShardProjectile.png` into `Content/Weapons/Flamerang/` (the boomerang ones the size of the vanilla Flamarang sprite, single frame) and they replace the vanilla textures.

Test checklist: left click throws at the cursor and only one can be out; thrown at open space it brakes, spins down and explodes at 20 tiles and two molten boomerangs arc out sideways and come back and are caught; thrown at an enemy or a wall it explodes at once, 6 obsidian shards fly up, fall and linger and hurt enemies that touch them, and one molten boomerang flies straight back; the explosion burns enemies; molten boomerangs leave a fiery trail that sets enemies on fire; you can throw again while the molten ones are still flying; shards are gone after about 8 s; Ice Boomerang, Enchanted Boomerang and Trimarang still work as before; two players: the other player sees the flight, the explosions, the shards, the molten arcs and trails and the hit sparks.

Pixel line patch (tested, all checks passed): the molten trail is now a `PixelTrail` (a pixel-snapped line that cools and shrinks with age, laid one point per tick) instead of separate squares. When the boomerang dies (caught), `OnKill` hands the trail to `DetachedTrails`, which keeps ticking and drawing it until every point has aged out, so the trail fades after the catch. The owner's machine keeps burning enemies that touch the detached trail, reaching less far the older the point is (`TrailBurnFade`). Visual state is per machine and never synced; burning goes through `NPC.AddBuff`. Tuning: `TrailWidth`, `TrailJitter`, `TrailBurnFade`, `TrailLifeTicks`. Detached trails are drawn after tiles, so they sit behind enemies and projectiles. Test: fire a molten boomerang and catch it: the trail must not vanish, it shrinks and cools away over about 40 ticks; an enemy touching the leftover trail still catches fire; the line has no gaps at high speed and no brighter overlap spots; two players: the other player sees the same fading trail.

Flamerang patch 2 (applied, untested): the open-space molten boomerangs no longer follow a fixed arc: they get the sideways impulse and then the new `BoomerangFlight.StepForceReturn` pulls them toward the player's current position (tuning `ReturnAcceleration` 1, `ReturnDrag` 0.96, `ReturnMaxSpeed` 20, `MoltenArcSpeed` 13; the contact one uses the same flight). Shards: `ShardMaxHits` 4, `ShardMaxAlive` 15, no more fade-out. Test: throw at open space, then walk or run sideways before the explosion and while the molten ones fly: they curve in a smooth arc toward where you are now and are caught; standing still gives two symmetrical arcs; a shard breaks (dark fragments, sparks, crack) on its 4th hit and when its 8 s end; throw at walls four times in a row: never more than 15 shards, the oldest break first; the trail still fades after the catch; two players: the other player sees the arcs, the shards and the breaking.

## 6. Shroomerang (vanilla `ItemID.Shroomerang`) [patches 1-2 applied, untested]

Design: it flies like the Enchanted Boomerang's normal throw: a fixed range of 20 tiles in the direction of the throw (braking over the last 70 px), a block ends the way out and turns it back, then a force-based return (`BoomerangFlight.StepForceReturn`, one smooth arc) and a catch. It pierces everything (an enemy can be hit again after 12 ticks, and again on the way back). Only one can be in the air; the mushrooms do not block the next throw. While it flies, in both directions, it drops a glowing mushroom every 8 ticks (`ShroomerangMushroomProjectile`, vanilla glowing mushroom sprite). A mushroom starts with a fifth of the boomerang's velocity plus a random push, spins in place and slows down gradually (drag 0.94 per tick). It ends in a soft cloud of glowing spores, not an explosion (`ShroomerangBurstProjectile`: a fat puff of blue smoke and glowing spores with no flash, rings or bang, only a quiet low pop; it still damages every enemy in a circle of radius 80 px once, 0.8x the boomerang's damage at the moment it was dropped) when its 2.5 s are over, or when an enemy touches it (a fresh mushroom ignores enemies for 20 ticks, so the boomerang cannot set off the mushroom it just dropped inside an enemy). At most 8 mushrooms per player: spawning the 9th makes the oldest explode at once. No camera shake. Look: blue glow and afterimages on the boomerang, mushrooms pop in with an overshoot, pulse, leave spores while they move and blink white faster and faster before they go off; the burst is the cloud puff above, with spores that keep drifting for a moment.

Shared code: `BoomerangFlight.StepOutbound` (range, brake, distance flown) was added to `Common/BoomerangFlight.cs` in this patch; the Wooden Boomerang and the Thorn Chakram patches use it, so apply this patch first. The Enchanted Boomerang and the Flamerang still have their own copy of that code; switching them over is an optional cleanup.

Multiplayer design: the owner's client decides the throw, the turn, a block hit, every mushroom spawn, the eviction of the oldest mushroom, the enemy-touch trigger and the burst spawn. The boomerang's phase (ai[0]) flips through netUpdate; the way out and the return run identically on every machine. Mushrooms and bursts are normal spawned projectiles (synced by tModLoader); a mushroom's explode flag (ai[0] = 1) is synced through netUpdate and every machine removes its own copy next tick. Spin, drag and lifetime are deterministic. The burst's damage goes through the normal owner-client hit path; every other machine plays its visuals from the synced spawn and adds hit sparks through `RemoteHitTracker`. No custom packets.

Art: optional. Drop `ShroomerangProjectile.png` into `Content/Weapons/Shroomerang/` (single frame, same size as the vanilla sprite) and it replaces the vanilla texture; the mushroom uses the vanilla glowing mushroom sprite on purpose.

Test checklist: throw goes out about 20 tiles, brakes and comes back in a smooth arc and is caught; a wall on the way out turns it back; it pierces a group of enemies and hits the same one again on the way back; mushrooms appear every 8 ticks on the way out and back, spin and slow down; a mushroom explodes after about 2.5 s and the burst hurts enemies in the circle once; an enemy walking into a mushroom sets it off (not in its first 20 ticks); with 8 mushrooms out the next spawn makes the oldest explode at once; no camera shake; only one boomerang in flight, and the next throw works while mushrooms are still out; two players: the other player sees the boomerang, the mushrooms, the blink, the burst and the hit sparks, and the burst's damage registers; the Enchanted Boomerang, Flamerang and Trimarang still behave as before. Unverified names to check first if the build complains: `ItemID.Shroomerang`, `ProjectileID.Shroomerang`. Patch 2: when a mushroom ends there is a soft puff cloud (blue smoke with glowing spores) with a quiet pop, no bang, no flash and no rings, and enemies inside it still take the one hit.

## 7. Wooden Boomerang (vanilla `ItemID.WoodenBoomerang`) [patches 1-2 applied, untested]

Design: it flies like the Enchanted Boomerang's normal throw: a fixed range of 14 tiles in the direction of the throw (braking over the last 60 px), then a force-based return and a catch. Only one can be in the air. The first enemy or block it hits breaks it in half (on the way back it passes through blocks, so only enemies break it there; `BreakOnTilesWhileReturning` in the tuning changes that): the boomerang is gone (no return, so the next throw works at once) and two shards (`WoodenBoomerangShardProjectile`) fly away from the break, one to each side of the break direction (after a block hit: back away from the block), with a little hop. The shards slow down in the air (drag 0.97), sink slowly and linger up to about 1.7 s, hurting every enemy that touches them (0.6x the boomerang's damage, the same enemy again after 20 ticks). A shard breaks in a puff of splinters after 3 hits, when it touches a block, or when its time is up; they do not fade out. Look: wood chips on the way, a burst of splinters and a crack on the break, chips while the shards fly. No camera shake.

Multiplayer design: the owner's client decides the throw, the turn and the break (OnHitNPC and the block test of the boomerang run there). ## Common: pixel line library

Use `Common/PixelLine.cs` for any pixel-snapped line or trail (never write a new line drawer). `PixelLine.Draw(points, t, count, grid, width, color)` draws a polyline on a world pixel grid; `t` is a per-point value (age or position) that is interpolated and passed to the `width(t)` and `color(t)` callbacks; alpha-0 colors are additive. For a trail laid by a projectile: subclass `PixelTrailStyle` (Grid, LifeTicks, Layers, Jitter, `GetWidth(layer, age)`, `GetColor(layer, age)`, optional `Update(trail)` for embers or burning), create `new PixelTrail(style, isOwner)` on every machine, call `trail.Add(position)` and `trail.Tick()` each tick and `trail.Draw()` in `PreDraw`, and in `OnKill` call `DetachedTrails.Hand(trail)` so it keeps fading. Do the damage or buff part only when `trail.IsOwner`. `IceFx.DrawPixel` stays as the single-square helper.

Sweep trails: use `Common/SweepTrail.cs` for a held sword's swing trail that should cover the blade, not only its tip. Subclass `SweepTrailStyle` (Grid, LifeTicks, Rails, `GetColor(across, age)` with across 0 = the outer/tip edge and 1 = the inner edge), create `new SweepTrail(style, isOwner)` on every machine, call `trail.Add(outerPoint, innerPoint)` a few times per tick while the blade swings (move the inner point from the tip toward the hand to make it expand), `Tick()` each tick, `Draw()` in `PreDraw`, and `DetachedTrails.Hand(trail)` in `OnKill`. First customer: the Ice Blade.

Afterimages: use `Common/AfterimageTrail.cs` (never loop over `Projectile.oldPos` in a PreDraw again). `AfterimageTrail.Sprite(texture, lifeTicks, minScale)` makes a trail of the projectile's own sprite, or `new AfterimageTrail(lifeTicks, painter)` for a custom shape (see the Ice Blade wave's crescents). Create it lazily on the client, then in `PostAI` (skip when `Main.dedServ` or the projectile is no longer active) call `trail.Tick()` and then `trail.Add(position, rotation, scale, color)` (not while the projectile is hidden); draw it with `trail?.Draw()` in `PreDraw`, before the body; and in `OnKill` call `DetachedTrails.Hand(trail)` and null the field. Every afterimage carries its own age, so after the projectile dies the leftover ones keep fading on their own (`DetachedTrails` draws them behind enemies and projectiles). `color` is the afterimage's brightest look (alpha 0 = additive); the painter fades it with age.

Held sword pose: use `Common/HeldSwordPose.cs` for any weapon whose held projectile is a vanilla sword sprite posed by the owner's arm (`ToWorld`, `Hand`, `BladeDirection`, `Sprite`); the Ice Blade and the Starfury use it. Never copy that math into a new weapon.
